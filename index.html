Awning math

The awning that cools the room in July is the same one that steals the free heat in January, and the fabric bill always includes a surprise. Awning math works in sun slopes - how many meters the sun drops for every meter it travels out, measured with a stick and its shadow - and turns your window into the depth that shades it in summer, the sun it still admits in winter, and the square meters of material to buy. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Depth plan - window, gap and summer slope become the projection you need.
  • Reach plan - that awning and a winter slope become shade and sun on the glass.
  • Fabric plan - depth, width and waste become square meters to buy.

Labeled norms

Measure a sun slope yourself: stand a vertical stick, divide its height by its shadow. Labeled summer slope: 1.9 (high sun). Labeled winter slope: 0.55 (low sun). Labeled waste: 10% for hems and seams. These are reference values, not rules - your wall, your window and your own shadow-stick decide the real numbers.
